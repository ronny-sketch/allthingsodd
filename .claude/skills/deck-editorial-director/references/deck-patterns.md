# Deck patterns

Choose the pattern that matches the audience and decision. Do not automatically use a startup pitch structure.

## 1. Strategic narrative deck

Use for: vision, organisational direction, ecosystem thesis, major internal/external strategy.

1. What is changing?
2. Why does it matter?
3. What is structurally missing?
4. What is our thesis?
5. What are we building / doing?
6. How do the pieces reinforce each other?
7. What proof do we have?
8. Where does this lead?
9. What needs to happen next?

## 2. Partnership / sales deck

Use for: corporate partners, sponsors, commercial collaborators.

1. The relevant opportunity for this audience
2. Why now
3. What we do
4. Why we are credible
5. Who / what they gain access to
6. Concrete ways to work together
7. Examples / proof
8. Proposed next step

Do not spend half the deck explaining the organisation before explaining why the partner should care.

## 3. Funding / public-interest deck

Use for: foundations, cities, ministries, public funding, institutional partners.

1. Public or sector-level problem
2. Evidence and urgency
3. Why current structures are insufficient
4. Proposed intervention
5. Activities / work packages
6. Target groups and participation
7. Expected outputs and outcomes
8. Measurement
9. Delivery capability and partners
10. Budget / sustainability / continuation
11. Ask

## 4. Project proposal deck

1. Brief / challenge
2. Our interpretation
3. Proposed concept
4. Experience / user journey / programme
5. Delivery model
6. Roles and responsibilities
7. Timeline
8. Budget
9. Risks / dependencies
10. Decision needed

## 5. Concept reveal deck

Use when introducing a new format, event, programme or brand architecture.

1. The tension or unmet need
2. The insight
3. The new concept in one sentence
4. What it is / what it is not
5. How it works
6. Who it is for
7. Example experience
8. Why this organisation can do it
9. What happens next

## 6. Evidence / impact deck

1. What we set out to test
2. What happened
3. Headline results
4. Who participated
5. What changed / emerged
6. Economic or cultural evidence
7. Case examples
8. Lessons
9. What we will change next
10. Implication / ask

## Slide-title rules

Prefer titles that carry the argument.

Topic title:

> Current state

Argument title:

> Creative activity is growing, but continuity still depends on short-term projects

Topic title:

> Our model

Argument title:

> Events, spaces and programmes solve different parts of the same continuity problem

Topic title:

> Partnership opportunities

Argument title:

> Partners can enter through visibility, programmes, space or co-created projects

## The 3-slide test

Take any three consecutive slides and read only their titles.

They should form a logical mini-argument. If they do not, the deck probably relies too much on body text to create the story.
