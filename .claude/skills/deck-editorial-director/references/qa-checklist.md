# High-stakes deck QA checklist

Use this for major partner, funding, board, investor or public-sector materials.

## Story

- Can the deck be summarised in one sentence?
- Is the audience's problem or opportunity visible early enough?
- Does each section answer a question raised by the previous section?
- Is there a clear shift from context to proposition to proof to action?
- Is the ending a consequence of the story rather than a generic CTA?

## Slide hierarchy

- Does every slide have one main job?
- Does the title state the point?
- Can the slide be understood in 5–10 seconds at presentation distance?
- Is important information visually prioritised in the copy?
- Is anything duplicated across adjacent slides?

## Language

- Remove generic modifiers that do not change meaning.
- Replace abstract nouns with concrete actors and actions.
- Break repeated “X, Y and Z” rhythms.
- Remove rhetorical filler such as “more than just”, “not only... but also...” and “in an ever-changing world”.
- Remove phrases that could appear in almost any organisation's deck.
- Check idiomatic Finnish / English rather than translation logic.

## Claims and evidence

- Are numbers sourced?
- Are conflicting numbers unresolved?
- Are plans labelled as plans?
- Are ambitions labelled as ambitions?
- Is causality overstated?
- Does every large claim have proof, mechanism or a clear rationale?

## Audience

- What would this audience challenge?
- What information are they likely to consider missing?
- Are their incentives visible?
- Does the deck answer “why you?”, “why now?” and “what exactly do you want from us?”

## Design readiness

- Can the copy breathe?
- Is a diagram better than another paragraph?
- Are tables only used when comparison is the actual point?
- Are numbers prominent when they carry the argument?
- Are long lists grouped into meaningful categories?

## Final red-team pass

Ask:

> If I were sceptical, where would I stop believing this deck?

Fix that point before polishing secondary wording.
